# Proposed book outline

This outline defines the intended learning sequence. Chapter titles and boundaries can change during review, but Level 1 should remain focused on foundations and belonging.

## Front matter: Welcome to skateboarding

- What skateboarding is and why people stay with it
- No single correct kind of skateboarder
- Progress, fear, failure, creativity, and fun
- How to use the book and its QR links
- Level 1 progression checklist

## 1. A brief history of skateboarding

- Sidewalk surfing and early boards
- Pools, vertical skating, and the first major scenes
- Skate videos and the Bones Brigade era
- The rise of street skating
- X Games, mainstream recognition, and the Olympics
- Women, adaptive skating, and international scenes
- A visual timeline connecting eras, riders, tricks, companies, and contests

The chapter should orient the reader without becoming a complete historical encyclopedia.

## 2. Anatomy of a skateboard

- Deck, grip tape, trucks, wheels, bearings, hardware, bushings, risers, and rails
- Nose, tail, concave, wheelbase, and dimensions
- How individual components affect the ride
- Exploded-board illustration and terminology map

## 3. Choosing your first setup

- Complete versus custom board
- Deck width, wheel size and hardness, truck size, and bearings
- Street, park, transition, cruising, and youth setups
- Shoes, helmets, pads, skate tools, and useful accessories
- Buying from a local shop and avoiding unsafe equipment
- A simple setup decision path

## 4. Building and maintaining your board

- Applying grip tape and installing components
- Adjusting trucks and breaking in bushings
- Rotating or replacing wheels
- Cleaning or replacing bearings
- Recognizing cracks, razor tail, worn parts, and loose hardware
- A pre-session safety check

## 5. Before your first trick

- Regular and goofy stance
- Foot position, balance, and posture
- Pushing, including the mongo conversation without ridicule
- Turning, carving, tic-tacs, and kick turns
- Stopping, running out, and rolling over rough ground
- Looking ahead and becoming comfortable at speed

Confident riding is treated as a real skill rather than filler before the ollie.

## 6. Falling, fear, and staying safer

- Running out, rolling, sliding, and protecting the wrists
- Warming up and recognizing fatigue
- Helmets and pads without judgment
- Common injuries and signs that require professional care
- Breaking intimidating goals into smaller evidence-building steps

## 7. Your first tricks

Every trick uses the same page system: definition, prerequisites, foot placement, movement, common mistakes, troubleshooting, drill, challenge, and TrickBook QR link.

Proposed Level 1 set:

1. Riding and pushing
2. Tic-tac
3. Kick turn
4. Manual foundations
5. Hippie jump
6. Body varial
7. Caveman
8. Boneless
9. Riding off a curb
10. Ollie
11. Frontside 180
12. Backside 180
13. Pop shove-it
14. Frontside shove-it
15. Fakie riding
16. Fakie revert
17. Rock to fakie on small transition
18. Drop-in preparation and first drop-in

The final trick list should be validated against TrickBook prerequisites and beginner progression data before drafting.

## 8. How tricks work

- Pop, scoop, flick, jump, catch, and landing
- Regular, fakie, switch, and nollie
- Frontside and backside
- Rotation and board control
- Flatground, ledge, rail, gap, and transition families
- Slides, grinds, grabs, and combinations
- How skateboarding trick names are constructed
- A visual trick-family map built from the TrickBook knowledge graph

## 9. Reading a skatepark

- Street plazas, bowls, pools, mini ramps, vert ramps, and flow parks
- Banks, hips, ledges, rails, stairs, gaps, quarter pipes, spines, and pyramids
- Lines, traffic, blind spots, and collision zones
- Choosing beginner-friendly terrain
- Inspecting obstacles and surfaces
- Indoor and outdoor park differences

## 10. Skatepark etiquette

- Watch before entering and wait for an opening
- Do not snake lines or sit on obstacles
- Retrieve loose boards quickly
- Respect different abilities and disciplines
- Advice, filming, wax, music, trash, and shared space
- Kids, scooters, BMX riders, and mixed-use parks
- What to do after a collision
- Posted rules versus unwritten rules

Recurring visual concept: **Etiquette Court**, where Mack considers ordinary park disputes.

## 11. Street skating

- What makes a street spot
- Spot terminology and terrain evaluation
- Pedestrians, traffic, property, businesses, and security
- Responding calmly when asked to leave
- Avoiding damage and unnecessary conflict
- Local laws and the limits of what the book can generalize
- Famous spots and why ordinary-looking terrain becomes culturally important

The book describes street culture honestly without encouraging trespassing, damage, or unsafe behavior.

## 12. Skateboarding culture

- Sessions, crews, and local skate shops
- Videos, filming, magazines, zines, music, art, fashion, and graphics
- DIY spots and community effort
- Sponsorship and what “getting sponsored” actually means
- Gatekeeping, authenticity, and supporting beginners
- Slang and regional differences
- Creativity and individual expression

## 13. Riders who shaped skateboarding

Riders are organized by their contributions rather than fame alone:

- Pioneers
- Vert innovators
- Architects of street skating
- Technical progression and style
- Women who reshaped the industry
- Adaptive skateboarders
- International innovators
- Emerging generations

Each short profile includes era, discipline, contribution, a defining moment, why the rider matters, and related TrickBook entries.

## 14. Brands, shops, and the skate industry

- Deck, truck, wheel, bearing, and shoe companies
- Core brands and large athletic brands
- Graphics and artist collaborations
- Teams, sponsorship, distribution, and manufacturing
- Why local skate shops matter
- Marketing claims and unsafe counterfeit equipment

Company coverage should be historically relevant and editorial, not advertising.

## 15. Contests and events

- Runs, jams, best trick, street, park, and transition formats
- Difficulty, execution, consistency, style, and use of course
- Heats, qualifiers, finals, and rankings
- Amateur, professional, community, and Olympic events
- Demos and video premieres
- Entering a first local contest
- Watching contests with a more informed eye

## 16. Filming your skateboarding

- Using video to understand progress
- Basic phone setup, orientation, framing, and angles
- Stationary and follow filming
- Keeping the filmer and other skaters safe
- Reviewing attempts constructively
- Simple editing, music rights, and responsible posting
- Documenting skating without letting social media control the session

## 17. Your first 30 sessions

- **Sessions 1–5:** Balance, pushing, turning, and stopping
- **Sessions 6–10:** Kick turns, reverts, and hippie jumps
- **Sessions 11–15:** Curb control and ollie preparation
- **Sessions 16–20:** Ollies and fakie movement
- **Sessions 21–25:** Shove-its, 180 preparation, and small ramps
- **Sessions 26–30:** Building lines and choosing personal goals

Each session combines a warm-up, riding skill, trick skill, culture or etiquette idea, and reflection prompt.

## 18. Where to go next

- Building an individual progression path
- Exploring street, transition, freestyle, cruising, or a mixture
- Finding local communities, shops, parks, and events
- Using TrickBook to log progress and explore related knowledge
- Level 2 preview
- Final Level 1 challenge: design and land a five-part line

## Reference section

- Illustrated glossary
- Trick-family map
- Setup chart and maintenance checklist
- Park obstacle guide and etiquette card
- Recommended books, films, magazines, and archives
- Trick, rider, brand, place, and event indexes
- Source notes and links to living TrickBook content

## Recurring page features

- **Kaori's Notes:** Clear connections, definitions, and knowledge maps
- **Mack's Call:** Practical judgment from lived skate experience
- **Try This:** A measurable exercise
- **What's Going Wrong?:** Troubleshooting flowcharts
- **Anatomy of a Slam:** Useful failure analysis with restrained humor
- **What That Means:** Skate terminology in context
- **Spot Check:** How to inspect terrain or an obstacle
- **Skate History Detour:** A short story connected to the current topic
- **Scan to Watch:** QR links to relevant TrickBook media

## Open review decisions

- Final title and subtitle
- Target age range and reading level
- Exact Level 1 trick list
- Approximate page count and price point
- Whether the 30-session plan is a chapter or a removable workbook section
- Rider, company, event, and spot selection criteria
- Digital-only launch versus simultaneous print edition
